Chimney math

Every fire leaves a film behind, warm air climbs because physics says so, and the flue has to match the hearth it serves. Chimney math turns a season of fires into grams of creosote on a labeled scale, flue height and temperatures into pascals of stack pull, and the fireplace opening into a round-liner size by the labeled one-tenth rule. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled - never an inspection.

The three tools

  • Sweep plan - fires a week, weeks since the last sweep, and a labeled creosote rate become grams of buildup and a labeled sweep verdict.
  • Draft plan - flue height plus inside and outside temperatures become pascals of stack pull on a labeled scale.
  • Flue plan - the opening's width and height become a round flue diameter by the labeled one-tenth rule.

Labeled norms and limits

Labeled creosote rates: 2 g a fire for seasoned hardwood, 4 g for softwood, 8 g for green wood - illustrative, since wood, stove and burn style move the number. The draft figure is a sea-level stack estimate at a labeled constant of 3460, not a measurement of a real flue. The one-tenth flue rule is a common labeled ratio, not a code. None of these tools inspects a chimney or judges one safe - creosote, blockages and carbon monoxide belong to a certified sweep, never to a calculator.
